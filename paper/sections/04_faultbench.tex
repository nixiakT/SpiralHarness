\section{HarnessFaultBench}
\label{sec:faultbench}

Real benchmarks rarely reveal whether a proposed patch touched the true cause
of failure.  The proposed HarnessFaultBench design complements them with
generated scenarios whose
oracle-correct harness, injected fault, causal surface, trigger, admissible
repair, exact revert, and neutral placebo are known before search.  Each
scenario also has disjoint exploration, gate, sealed, and protected-clean
groups.  The confirmatory generator code, design matrix, and sampling target
must be committed before any scored execution.

The current executable v4 artifact is a deterministic trust-closure fixture
with seven generated fault families across six declared surfaces.  Each
partition has four scenario roles per family, hence 28 scenarios, but those 28
scenarios are not 28 independent families.  Trusted deterministic middleware,
not the charged base-model output, determines final behavior.  The fixture does
not support a live-model, LLM-solver, optimizer-capability, self-evolution, or
powered-benchmark claim, and it does not realize the full factor table below.

A \emph{verified repair} requires sealed target improvement, protected-slice
non-inferiority, candidate--revert and candidate--placebo attribution, and
passing activation and adherence evidence.  Localization accuracy alone is
insufficient.  Primary fault-suite metrics are verified-repair probability,
accepted-edit precision, false-promotion probability on null/unrepairable
families, harmful commits, and calls or tokens per verified repair.

Programmatic link checks are primary.  Any model-judged mechanism endpoint uses
an arm-blinded frozen judge and must pass disjoint human-label calibration with
prespecified agreement and class-conditional error thresholds; otherwise it is
inconclusive.

The null-family count is powered independently of search-seed repeats.  If the
family-level events are independent, exchangeable draws from a prospectively
defined target distribution, observing no false promotions in at least 59 null
or unrepairable families puts the one-sided 95\% exact binomial upper bound
below 5\%.  The current seven-family fixture does not meet that target, and
multiple roles or search seeds from one family do not create new independent
families.  Without a defensible sampling target, we report only the frozen-roster
rate and do not interpret the binomial bound as population coverage.
Synthetic faults cannot by themselves establish real-task usefulness; they
identify gate operating characteristics under known ground truth, while the
real-task study tests external validity.
