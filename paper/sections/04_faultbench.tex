\section{HarnessFaultBench}
\label{sec:faultbench}

Real benchmarks rarely reveal whether a proposed patch touched the true cause
of failure.  The proposed HarnessFaultBench design complements them with
generated scenarios whose
oracle-correct harness, injected fault, causal surface, trigger, admissible
repair, exact revert, and neutral placebo are known before search.  Each
scenario also has disjoint exploration, gate, sealed, and protected-clean
groups.  The confirmatory generator code, design matrix, and sampling target
must be committed before any scored execution.

The current executable v4 artifact is a deterministic trust-closure fixture
with seven generated fault families across six declared surfaces.  Each
partition has four scenario roles per family, hence 28 scenarios, but those 28
scenarios are not 28 independent families.  Trusted deterministic middleware,
not the charged base-model output, determines final behavior.  The fixture does
not support a live-model, LLM-solver, optimizer-capability, self-evolution, or
powered-benchmark claim, and it does not realize the full factor table below.
A separate public-development runner now lets one frozen-model interface call
choose a finite repair and scores untouched model outputs for the complete
parent--candidate--revert--placebo quartet.  A four-context fixture-backed path
fits all (SS/MS/SM/MM) candidates on one paired exploration block, freezes
them jointly, and then runs every quartet on one source/group-disjoint fixture
GATE block before computing the four automatic gates.  Its single
\(4\times(28+1+4\times28)=116+448=564\)-call ledger and raw outputs are
offline replayable; contexts use a fixed SS--MS--SM--MM order rather than the
counterbalancing required for confirmation.  This closes development
plumbing only: no live provider, powered family/search replication, independent
freeze-time or one-time-consumption attestation, cross-process atomicity, or
sealed result exists.

The prospective design crosses mutable surface, causal stage, fault topology,
distractors, distribution shift, and repairability under a compatibility mask;
the full factor table is in Appendix~\ref{app:fault-design}.

Generator ownership is itself a validity threat.  The powered roster therefore
must add a blinded, naturally occurring fault subset and an external adversarial
audit; define admissible repairs as semantic equivalence classes rather than one
blessed patch; adjudicate novel legal repairs without condition or score
visibility; and calibrate that placebo artifacts preserve the declared behavior
and byte/token envelope.  Shared templates are clustered at their source family,
and model-judged links cannot be validated by the optimizing model's own
uncalibrated preference.

A \emph{verified repair} requires sealed target improvement, protected-slice
non-inferiority, candidate--revert and candidate--placebo attribution, and
passing activation and adherence evidence.  Localization accuracy alone is
insufficient.  The co-primary operating characteristic is family-level false-
promotion probability on a frozen null/unrepairable target, both its absolute
upper bound under \fullarm{} and its reduction relative to \scorearm{}.
Verified-repair probability, harmful-promotion probability, accepted-edit
precision, inconclusive rate, and calls or tokens per verified repair are
reported alongside it.  Precision is never treated as favorable when there are
no accepted edits.

Programmatic link checks are primary.  Any model-judged mechanism endpoint uses
an arm-blinded frozen judge and must pass disjoint human-label calibration with
prespecified agreement and class-conditional error thresholds; otherwise it is
inconclusive.

The null-family count is powered on independent family clusters, each assigned
one preregistered primary optimizer seed shared across policies.  Under
independent exchangeable sampling, 59 zero-event families suffice only for an
\emph{unadjusted} one-sided 95\% exact upper bound below 5\%; that familiar
calculation is not the present co-primary sample-size rule.  The final count
must use the preregistered multiplicity allocation, paired FULL--SCORE contrast,
family-level dependence, configured failures, and power target.  A stratified
fractional-factorial roster additionally requires frozen stratum weights and a
coverage-validated clustered interval; it cannot borrow the exchangeable exact-
binomial calculation.  The current seven-family fixture is insufficient, and
multiple roles or seeds from one family do not create new families.  Without a
defensible sampling target, only a frozen-roster rate is reportable.
Synthetic faults cannot by themselves establish real-task usefulness; they
identify gate operating characteristics under known ground truth, while the
real-task study tests external validity.
