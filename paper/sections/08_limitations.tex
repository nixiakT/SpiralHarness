\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

First, confirmatory evidence is not yet available; all performance, causal, and
generality conclusions remain blocked.  Second, controlled faults make causal
surfaces observable but may underrepresent the ambiguity and interactions of
real harness failures.  Third, activation and adherence probes can themselves
perturb behavior, and a passed probe is not proof that every downstream gain
came from the declared mechanism.  Fourth, inference applies to a fixed roster
of models and tasks rather than all future systems.  Fifth, the present logical
separation uses in-process capabilities; hostile candidate code requires an
out-of-process evaluator, sandbox, asymmetric signing, secret isolation, and
atomic release service.  Finally, mechanism gating adds calls and latency.  It
is useful only if its reduction in harmful or false promotions justifies that
cost, which is an empirical question rather than a premise.
