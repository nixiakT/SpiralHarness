Language models increasingly rewrite their own prompts, tools, memory, and
control flow.  The unresolved decision is not whether an edit can be generated,
but \emph{when an adaptively selected edit can be committed credibly}.  A score
increase may reflect selection noise, an inactive instruction, a grader
shortcut, or a hidden regression; repeated promotion tests can also overfit the
gate itself.  We introduce \systemname, a training-free protocol that treats
commit authorization as a separate statistical decision.  The protocol requires
an authority outside the mutable harness to evaluate typed edits through matched
child, parent, exact-revert, and neutral-placebo interventions, then check
activation, adherence, intended behavior, utility, protected behavior, and cost
under a campaign-level error budget.  Our prospective headline is deliberately conjunctive: the full
policy must control and reduce family-level false promotion, improve one-time
sealed utility over score-only search and the unmodified harness, and pass
protected-behavior and cost non-inferiority.  Thus a reject-all gate cannot
succeed.  A prospective controlled-fault factorial is designed to estimate
mechanism-feedback, promotion-rule, and interaction effects.  All empirical conclusions remain
\evidenceblocked{the protocol is pilot-pending and neither the powered fault
roster nor the independent searches and atomic sealed audit have been
completed}.  We report the boundary explicitly rather than treating
development pilots as evidence of credible self-evolution.
