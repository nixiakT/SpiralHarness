\section{Prospective Evaluation Design}
\label{sec:experiments}

Before confirmatory search, an immutable preregistration will freeze the design
and answer three primary questions: Does the joint mechanism-visible,
mechanism-gated policy outperform a score-only, performance-gated policy under
an otherwise identical budget?  Does it improve over the unmodified harness?
Does it increase verified repair on controlled faults?  The current protocol is
\evidenceblocked{pilot-pending rather than preregistered}; its margins, roster,
replication count, and analysis must be frozen before any main-study outcome.

\subsection{Conditions and matched budgets}

\purearm{} is an evaluation reference, not a search policy.  Within each
model--task--search-seed block it shares the exact provider-resolved solver
revision, inference configuration, task/group/rollout coordinates, and scoring
contract with every other arm.  Only provider-required serialization and the
task payload are permitted.  Its lack of optimizer calls is measured rather
than padded into fictitious search use; all actual evaluation usage is charged.
The single-call \purearm{} contrast measures effectiveness, not equal model budget.
A separate \(\textsc{Pure@B}\) reference spends FULL's frozen model-call/token
ceiling on independent provider-minimal samples combined by a task-native,
nonadaptive rule fixed without grader or sealed feedback.  The cross-task
contrast is defined only if every primary task freezes such a total rule.

Within every model--task--seed block, \scorearm{} and \fullarm{} share the seed
harness, proposer and sampling settings, editable surfaces, proposals per
round, solver, task and rollout coordinates, nomination rule, number of gate
queries, and hard resource ceilings.  Cache state is neutralized or charged
symmetrically.  All proposer, diagnoser, solver, grader, retry, and failed-call
usage is retained.  The primary same-model condition additionally requires one
exact provider-resolved model revision for every model-mediated role.  A role
may have a preregistered decoding configuration, but the role mapping and every
such configuration are identical across policies; any stronger external
proposer is analyzed only as a separate harness-optimization baseline.

The planned controlled-fault study uses a $2\times2$ decomposition that crosses
the optimizer view (score-only or mechanism-visible) with the promotion rule
(performance-only or mechanism-gated).  Its SS, MS, SM, and MM cells estimate
conditional guidance and promotion-policy simple effects plus their interaction;
MM is \fullarm{} and SS is \scorearm{}.  The authority freezes all nominations
at index $q$ before opening their shared fresh block; isolated contexts are
counterbalanced by a verifier-owned seed.  Treatment-induced candidates may
differ, so the coefficients are adaptive-policy intention-to-treat effects, not
candidate-level mediation.  A shuffled mechanism packet is the information-
volume placebo.  Reportability requires runtime closure over all four cells,
receipts, failures, and one-time block consumption.

\paragraph{External harness optimizers.}
Secondary comparisons include Meta-Harness-style and Self-Harness optimizers
\citep{lee2026metaharness,zhang2026selfharness}.  Missing executable components
are labeled paper-algorithm reconstructions, never official reproductions.
Stronger role models make a baseline heterogeneous and ineligible for the
same-model or $>10$-point claim.  PenguinHarness's public recursive example
\citep{prismshadow2026penguin} remains exploratory unless its auditable
multi-task suite and graders are released before the freeze.

\subsection{Models, tasks, and independent runs}

The prospective roster targets
\designvalue{three open model configurations spanning at least two families}
and \designvalue{four task families}: mathematical generation, multi-type
reasoning, high-label classification with group separation, and strict
structured generation or coding with source/time separation.  Availability
smoke tests may select deployable endpoints but may not inspect benchmark
scores.  Every arm within a cell uses the same exact model revision and
inference configuration.

One complete search is the repeated algorithmic unit.  The number of independent
search seeds is \evidenceblocked{a closed disjoint pilot, calibrated analysis,
and attested input closure are incomplete}.  Rollout repeats do not count as
independent search replications.  The repository's analytic proxy and basic
nested-bootstrap prototype cannot select a formal seed count or produce
confirmatory evidence (Appendix~\ref{app:power-proxy}).

\subsection{Estimands and analysis}

For fixed model and task rosters \(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{T}\), each primary unit
has a frozen programmatic binary-success rubric and source-group weighting.
Then \(\mu(a,m,t)\) averages over that target and the frozen randomization, so
0.10 is ten success-probability points rather than arbitrary normalization;
incompatible official metrics are secondary.  Cells receive equal weight:
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\mathrm{policy}}
  &= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}||\mathcal{T}|}
     \sum_{m,t}\left[\mu(\text{\fullarm},m,t)
       -\mu(\text{\scorearm},m,t)\right],\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{e2e}}
  &= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}||\mathcal{T}|}
     \sum_{m,t}\left[\mu(\text{\fullarm},m,t)
       -\mu(\text{\staticarm},m,t)\right].
\end{align}
The prospective secondary bare-model reference is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{pure}}
  = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}||\mathcal{T}|}
    \sum_{m,t}\left[\mu(\text{\fullarm},m,t)
      -\mu(\text{\purearm},m,t)\right].
\end{equation}
Define \(\Delta_{\mathrm{pure@B}}\) analogously for \(\textsc{Pure@B}\).
Here \(\Delta_{\mathrm{policy}}\) is the joint MM--SS deployable-policy effect;
it does not by itself isolate feedback visibility or promotion verification.
The third estimand is the \fullarm{}-minus-\scorearm{} difference in
verified-repair probability on HarnessFaultBench.  The prospective superiority
margins are 0.02 absolute binary-success probability (two percentage points)
for each real-task contrast and 0.15 for verified repair.  The three one-sided nulls put the corresponding effect at
or below its margin and use Holm--Bonferroni family-wise \(\alpha=0.05\)
\citep{holm1979simple}.  The primary statistical pass requires all three
Holm-corrected one-sided margin tests to reject.  A full headline conclusion
additionally requires every protected-behavior, cost, provenance, and leakage
constraint to close.  Their exact margins, missingness rules, and multiplicity
families remain \evidenceblocked{delegated design values that must be frozen
before confirmatory search}.  Verified repair will be weighted equally over the
eventual frozen model--repair-stratum cells.

The method-development selector combines all three Holm SESOI rejections with
simultaneous lower bounds above 0.10 for the two real-task contrasts.  It omits
protected, cost, provenance, leakage, PURE, PURE@B, and external-claim power, so it
cannot freeze sample size.  An effect on a null margin has only calibrated
type-I rejection probability, not 0.90 power.
Any $>10$-percentage-point statement, including
\(\Delta_{\mathrm{pure}}\) or \(\Delta_{\mathrm{pure@B}}\), requires every preregistered claim-family
simultaneous multiplicity-adjusted one-sided lower confidence bound to be
strictly greater than 0.10.  This is not the verified-repair primary margin,
which remains 0.15.

The current method-development prototype uses the declared equal-cell macro
estimand and a basic nested bootstrap that resamples search seeds within
model--task cells, task groups within runs, and repeats within groups.  It has
not completed coverage calibration, been frozen as the confirmatory estimator,
or consumed an authority-attested sealed dataset.  It therefore cannot freeze
the formal seed count or license a result.  The final preregistration must bind
one validated estimator with no outcome-selected fallback.  Failure-as-zero is
the intended intention-to-treat analysis, accompanied by complete-case and
worst-case sensitivity analyses.  Individual model, task, surface, and factor
findings are corrected or labeled exploratory.

\subsection{Selection closure, sealing, and cost}

Search stops at the first frozen round, proposal, nomination, optimizer-call,
resource, patience, or invalid-candidate limit.  All configured cells,
including failures, must close before the sealed authority accepts champion
hashes.  It evaluates the entire grid in one release.  A post-unseal change
starts a new preregistration and new sealed lineage.

We report score and protected outcomes alongside uncached and cached input,
output/reasoning tokens, normalized frozen-price cost, actual billed cost when
available, wall time, tool calls, failures, and retries.  Score per million
tokens and a Pareto frontier complement absolute outcomes; cost is not hidden
inside the optimized objective.
